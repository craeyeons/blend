\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The construction of Sections~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:hybrid} rests
on two structural claims that neither the theory nor the literature
settles on its own, and the experiments are organised around them.
The first is proxy faithfulness: the dimensionless score $\rho^\theta$
of \eqref{eq:rho-def}, built from the residual $\mathfrak{r}^\theta$
and the linearised error-transport estimate $e^\theta$, must track
the unobserved pointwise PINN error $\|h(\cdot)-u^\star(\cdot)\|$ in
the sense of Assumption~\ref{ass:linearisation} closely enough that
minimising the surrogate \eqref{eq:surrogate} reduces the hybrid PDE
error \eqref{eq:hybrid-bound}, not merely the proxy loss the rejector
is trained against. The second is geometric portability: although
the rejector returns a geometry-dependent partition
$\widehat{\Omega}_P\sqcup\widehat{\Omega}_C$ of $\Omega$, the mapping
it learns from local physical signals to abstention decisions should
transfer across geometries, so that a single trained rejector handles
$(PINN,\text{geometry})$ pairs it has not seen at training time. If
either claim fails, the framework collapses to a-posteriori
refinement performed at deployment, and the amortisation argument of
Section~\ref{sec:intro} disappears with it.

We test both claims on steady incompressible Navier--Stokes flow past
a cylinder at Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}=100$. The problem is
chosen for a specific reason: PINNs in this regime fail
\emph{non-uniformly}, with high-error regions concentrated in the
boundary layer along the cylinder and the recirculating wake
\citep{rao2020physics,wang2022whenpinns,krishnapriyan2021}. A
uniformly accurate or uniformly inaccurate PINN would tell us nothing
about whether the rejector can localise, whereas here the geometry
dictates where the error lives and the rejector's decisions admit a
direct visual and quantitative check. A linear-elliptic Poisson
problem with manufactured sharp features, which isolates the proxy
from the nonlinear coupling effects of NSE, is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:poisson} under the same protocol.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{PINN and rejector.}
For each cylinder geometry $(x_c,y_c,r)$ we train a separate PINN
$h=\widehat{u}_\theta$ to convergence at that geometry, with parabolic
inflow $u_0=1.0$, no-slip on the cylinder, and a standard outflow
condition on the right boundary. A dedicated PINN per geometry
matches the deployment scenario of Section~\ref{sec:intro}, in which
the predictor is fixed and only the rejector is learned, and prevents
the rejector from inheriting an artificial advantage by compensating
for a single under-trained joint PINN. The rejector itself is a small
convolutional network whose input channels are the four quantities
called out by the theory: the PINN field $h$, the residual
$\mathfrak{r}^\theta$, the error-transport estimate $e^\theta$ from
\eqref{eq:ete}, and a binary mask of the cylinder geometry. We
minimise the surrogate \eqref{eq:surrogate} with constant cost
$c(x)\equiv 1.1$. This value is fixed by construction rather than
tuned: the per-summand normalisation in \eqref{eq:rho-def} makes
$\rho^\theta$ a dimensionless field with median in $[1,2]$ on every
PDE family and every grid, so a cost slightly above unit median
selects an operating coverage in the upper tail of $\rho^\theta$ -- the
regime in which the proxy is meant to flag points -- without
problem-specific tuning. We add an anisotropic total-variation
penalty $\lambda_{\mathrm{tv}}\TV(s)$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{tv}}=1$,
which realises the regularised rejector class $\Hcal_r$ of
Section~\ref{sec:hybrid-subdomain}: $\widehat{\Omega}_C$ is biased
toward a finite union of solver-friendly connected regions and away
from the disconnected level-set geometry of the unrestricted optimum,
while the consistency guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:h-consistency}
is preserved. The PINN $h$ is held frozen throughout rejector training.

\paragraph{Hybrid pipeline and ground truth.}
At inference we proceed as in \eqref{eq:hybrid}. We first evaluate
the trained rejector on every fluid cell, which splits the domain
into a PINN region $\widehat{\Omega}_P$ and a solver region
$\widehat{\Omega}_C$. We then run a standard finite-volume
incompressible Navier--Stokes solver
\citep{leveque2007finite,brenner2008mathematical} on
$\widehat{\Omega}_C$, using the PINN values at the interface
$\Gamma$ as Dirichlet boundary data; the original physical boundary
conditions act on the rest of $\partial\widehat{\Omega}_C$ as usual.
The hybrid field is then formed by keeping the PINN on
$\widehat{\Omega}_P\cup\Gamma$ and the solver output on
$\widehat{\Omega}_C\setminus\Gamma$. The interface cells stay on
the PINN side because they are the cells that supplied the boundary
data; overwriting them would discard exactly the values the solver
was conditioned on.

The reference $u^\star$ is produced by the \emph{same} solver run
on the full domain $\Omega$. This is the strongest available control
in this kind of comparison: every error we report below is between
$\widehat{u}_{\mathrm{hyb}}$ (or $h$) and the solver the hybrid uses
internally, so improvements cannot be attributed to a mismatch
between two different reference codes.

\paragraph{Error metric.}
For a candidate field $u=(u_x,u_y,p)$ and the reference
$u^\star=(u_x^\star,u_y^\star,p^\star)$ on the shared discretisation
grid $\Omega_{\mathrm{grid}}\subset\Omega$ with $N=|\Omega_{\mathrm{grid}}|$
fluid cells, we report
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE}(u,u^\star)
  \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\,
  \frac{1}{N}\!\!\sum_{x\in\Omega_{\mathrm{grid}}}\!\!
  \Bigl[
    \bigl(u_x(x)-u_x^\star(x)\bigr)^2
    +\bigl(u_y(x)-u_y^\star(x)\bigr)^2
    +\bigl\|\nabla p(x)-\nabla p^\star(x)\bigr\|^2
  \Bigr]\,}.
  \label{eq:rmse}
\end{equation}
The form of \eqref{eq:rmse} is dictated by the gauge structure of the
problem. The velocity components enter as raw differences because the
boundary data -- parabolic inflow, no-slip on the cylinder, outflow on
the right -- pin the velocity gauge, leaving no admissible constant
offset. The pressure, by contrast, is determined by the steady
incompressible Navier--Stokes equations only up to an additive
constant, so two valid solutions $h$ and $u^\star$ may differ by a
global pressure offset of no physical content; reporting the raw
pressure difference would inflate the metric by a gauge artefact the
rejector cannot see or control. Replacing $p$ with $\nabla p$ removes
the gauge while retaining the only physically meaningful component of
the pressure field. We compute the gradient by second-order central
differences on $\Omega_{\mathrm{grid}}$, with one-sided stencils on
the boundary, and rescale by the grid spacing so that the gradient
term shares units with $u_x,u_y$ and $\mathrm{RMSE}$ is a single
scalar in velocity units. The same metric drives the coverage curves
of Figure~\ref{fig:cyl-coverage} and the per-axis panels of
Figure~\ref{fig:cyl-solution}.

\paragraph{Training and test geometries.}
The rejector is trained jointly on seven cylinder geometries that
bracket a held-in test triple along each axis
($x_c\in\{0.30,0.50,0.70,1.00\}$, $y_c\in\{0.30,0.40,0.50,0.80\}$,
$r\in\{0.10,0.12,0.15\}$); the bracketed triple $(0.60,0.55,0.11)$ is
absent from training. Each geometry is paired with its own PINN, so
at evaluation time the rejector sees a \emph{(PINN, geometry)} pair it
has not encountered, even though every component is individually
well-defined. Bracketing rather than extrapolating is the relevant
test of the portability claim: since the rejector reads only local
physical signals and inherits the smoothness of $\Hcal_r$,
interpolation inside the bracket distinguishes a residual-to-error
mapping that has been \emph{learned} from one that has been memorised
geometry by geometry. We complement this in-bracket triple with two
out-of-bracket stress configurations. The first, $r=0$ at $(0.5,0.5)$,
has no obstacle and tests whether the rejector degenerates gracefully
when the residual is uniformly small or instead routes the bulk to
the solver. The second, $r=0.2$ at $(0.5,0.8)$, places the cylinder
edge against the top wall and tests whether the rejector still
localises when the boundary layer overlaps a domain wall.
Table~\ref{tab:cyl-train} lists all ten configurations.

\subsection{The abstention loss as the deployment-time metric}
\label{sec:exp-cylinder}

The natural performance metric for the hybrid is the abstention loss
itself, evaluated on the partition the rejector returns:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{L}(s)
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{|\Omega_{\mathrm{grid}}|}
  \sum_{x\in\Omega_{\mathrm{grid}}}\!
  \Bigl[
    \rho^\theta(x)\,\ind\{s(x)\leq 0\}
    \;+\;c\,\ind\{s(x)>0\}
  \Bigr].
  \label{eq:abst-empirical}
\end{equation}
Two facts make $\widehat{L}$ the right object to read. First, by
Assumption~\ref{ass:linearisation},
$\rho^\theta\sim\|h-u^\star\|/m_u$ pointwise, so the accept side of
\eqref{eq:abst-empirical} is, up to the linearisation slack, an
average of the true PINN error over the kept region; the abstain
side is the explicit budget on solver volume. The abstention loss is
therefore not just a training surrogate -- it is a (proxy-based)
summary of the same hybrid error that an RMSE against $u^\star$
would report, with the solver cost exposed as an interpretable
second term. Second, $\widehat{L}$ is computable at deployment from
the trained PINN alone: no reference solution is needed. This is the
operational point of the whole framework. A practitioner who has
only $h$ on a new geometry can read $\widehat{L}$ off the rejector,
sweep $\tau$, and select the operating point $\tau^\star$ that
minimises it -- without ever running a reference solve.

For this to be useful we need to verify, once, that the abstention
loss is faithful: that the operating point chosen by minimising
$\widehat{L}$ agrees with the operating point one would have chosen
by minimising the true hybrid error if a reference were available.
This is the only place RMSE has to enter the experimental story.
Figure~\ref{fig:cyl-coverage} sweeps the threshold $\tau$ across the
score range on the test geometry and plots, against coverage
$|\widehat{\Omega}_C|/|\Omega|$, both $\widehat{L}$ -- which the
rejector sees -- and $\mathrm{RMSE}(\widehat{u}_{\mathrm{hyb}},
u^\star)$ from \eqref{eq:rmse} -- which it does not. Both curves
are U-shaped. The U in $\widehat{L}$ comes directly from
\eqref{eq:abst-empirical}: as coverage grows, the accept term falls
because high-$\rho^\theta$ points are absorbed into
$\widehat{\Omega}_C$, while the abstain term grows linearly at rate
$c$, and the sum is convex with a unique interior minimum at the
coverage where the marginal $\rho^\theta$ crosses $c$. The U in
RMSE comes from the hybrid bound \eqref{eq:hybrid-bound}: the
left-hand fall reflects high-error regions being absorbed into
$\widehat{\Omega}_C$, and the right-hand rise reflects the factor
$1+C_s$ amplifying the PINN error on the interface $\Gamma$ as
$\widehat{\Omega}_P$ becomes spatially scattered at high coverage --
a structural artefact of one-way Dirichlet coupling
(Section~\ref{sec:hybrid-couple}) that would persist even if the
rejector were Bayes-optimal.

The two minima sit at essentially the same coverage, $\approx 47\%$.
This co-location is the only RMSE-based statement we need.
Theorem~\ref{thm:h-consistency} guarantees that small abstention
excess implies small \emph{true} hybrid excess up to the
linearisation slack $\delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^\theta$, and the
alignment of the two minima is the empirical confirmation that this
slack is small enough at this PINN to make $\widehat{L}$ a faithful
deployment-time stand-in for the unobservable RMSE. From this point
on, every operating decision and every cross-configuration
comparison can be read off $\widehat{L}$ alone, and we report RMSE
in the table that follows only as a redundancy check rather than as
the primary signal.

\paragraph{Solution fields.}
At the operating point $\tau^\star$ selected by minimising
$\widehat{L}$, the hybrid restores the two pieces of physical
structure the PINN smears: the boundary-layer pressure gradient
adjacent to the cylinder and the wake separation behind it. The
rejector contour overlaid on the hybrid panel of
Figure~\ref{fig:cyl-solution} localises $\widehat{\Omega}_C$ around
the cylinder and the near wake -- exactly the regions where
$\rho^\theta$ is large -- and leaves the bulk upstream and the far
field on the PINN, which is what the deployment-time selection
criterion was supposed to deliver. The hybrid runs at $1.48\times$
wall-clock speedup over a full solver run on the same grid and
hardware; the rejector itself is essentially free, a single forward
pass through a small CNN evaluated once per inference. The
corresponding RMSE reduction from $1.15\!\times\!10^{-1}$ to
$2.27\!\times\!10^{-2}$ is reported in Table~\ref{tab:cyl} as a
redundancy check.

\subsection{Does a single rejector generalise across geometries?}
\label{sec:exp-generalisation}

A rejector trained jointly on the seven training geometries must
function as a routing rule on each of them and on the unseen test
geometries; otherwise the framework reduces to a-posteriori
refinement performed afresh at every deployment, and the
amortisation argument disappears. The claim is non-trivial because
$\widehat{\Omega}_C$ is a different region of $\Omega$ in each
configuration, and the rejector outputs a partition of a continuous
domain rather than a discrete label. Two failure modes would be
visible in the table below: a collapse of the abstention loss
$\widehat{L}^\star:=\widehat{L}(s_{\tau^\star})$ to one of the
extremes -- the pure-accept value $\E[\rho^\theta]$ or the pure-abstain
value $c$ -- with the corresponding collapse of the coverage to $0$
or $1$. We report $\widehat{L}^\star$ as the primary metric and
keep $\mathrm{RMSE}$ as a redundant check, in line with the
abstention-loss-faithfulness conclusion of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-cylinder}.

Neither failure mode appears. The abstention loss
$\widehat{L}^\star$ at the operating point $\tau^\star$ is bounded
strictly between the two extreme values $\E[\rho^\theta]$
(pure-accept) and $c=1.1$ (pure-abstain) on every configuration, and
the corresponding coverages cluster in the $38\text{--}50\%$ band --
the rejector consistently identifies a non-trivial high-error
subdomain rather than collapsing in either direction. The
in-bracket test row (row~8) sits inside the same band of
$\widehat{L}^\star$ as the training rows and at coverage
$47.7\%$ versus a training mean of $\approx 45\%$; a rejector that
had memorised the seven training decisions geometry by geometry
would either produce an outlier $\widehat{L}^\star$ on row~8 or
cluster with the worst training rows, and it does neither. This is
the direct reading of genuine interpolation across the training
bracket, made on the metric the rejector actually optimises.

The RMSE columns confirm the same story without adding a new one,
which is the role they play here. Hybrid RMSE is several-fold below
PINN RMSE everywhere, and the spread in hybrid RMSE tracks the
spread in PINN RMSE rather than geometry, exactly as
\eqref{eq:hybrid-bound} predicts: the term
$\|h-u^\star\|_{\widehat{\Omega}_P\cup\Gamma}$ is controlled by the
rejector only through the partition, and the hybrid cannot drag a
poor PINN below the floor set by its own kept region. End-to-end
wall-clock falls in the $1.5\text{--}1.9\times$ speedup band against
the full solver. The two out-of-bracket stress rows (rows~9--10) are
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, where we also take up
transfer to qualitatively different boundary conditions.
